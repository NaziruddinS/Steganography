What is Steganography?

Steganography is the practice of concealing a message, image, or file within another message, image, or file. Unlike cryptography, where the existence of the message is evident but not readable, steganography hides the very existence of the message. It comes from the Greek words "steganos" (covered) and "graphia" (writing), which literally means "covered writing."

The most common use of steganography in the modern digital era is to embed secret data within images, audio, video files, or other file types in such a way that only the intended recipient knows about the hidden content. This technique is widely used in secure communication, watermarking, and digital forensics.

Steganography has been used for centuries for covert communication, but with today's technology, its applications have grown. Whether you need to hide confidential data or share sensitive information securely, steganography offers a creative and effective solution.
